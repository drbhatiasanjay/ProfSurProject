# Pre-IPO equity decision support: research foundation

Research date: 7 November 2026. Purpose: support the incremental product requirements, not certify any issuer or recommend a security. All product design implications below are synthesis/inferences from the cited sources, not claims that those sources prescribe this application.

## Findings and product implications

### 1. Organize around an investment thesis, not a collection of ratios

Equity research connects business economics, competition, forecasts, valuation and risks. Investor guidance also directs attention to offering terms, dilution and selling shareholders. The product implication is a question-led synthesis above existing workpapers, with an explanation of what the price assumes. Sources: S01–S04.

### 2. Company quality, price and investor fit are separate assessments

A strong business can be expensive; a low multiple can reflect poor economics. The product must preserve the distinction between business assessment, valuation judgment, transaction terms and an investor's own constraints. A persona alone cannot establish suitability. Sources: S02–S04. The separation is a product inference, not an empirically calibrated investment rule.

### 3. Commentary should connect drivers to consequences

The revised IFRS commentary framework connects six areas of business information. Use it as a narrative design reference. Build observable-driver → financial consequence → decision implication explanations, with explicit uncertainty. This framework is not automatically a mandatory Indian prospectus standard. Source: S05.

### 4. Forecasts must reflect business economics and missing information

Forecast technique depends on business model, cyclicality and available information. Young-company valuation needs explicit treatment of short histories, losses, reinvestment and uncertainty. The product should offer driver-based scenarios and justified valuation methods, not extrapolate every historical CAGR or produce P/E for losses. Sources: S03, S06, S07.

### 5. Financial flexibility matters beyond debt ratios

Graham and Harvey survey 392 CFOs on finance decisions, including flexibility and equity dilution. This supports presenting financing trade-offs; it does not establish a particular hedge fraction, capex hurdle or issuer debt limit. Credit assessment also considers business and management risk, prospective cash and liquidity. Sources: S08, S09.

### 6. PE and strategic investors need a distinct transaction layer

Practitioner diligence covers commercial and operating questions, value-creation opportunities and exit routes. Product inference: public-offer analysis and private/pre-IPO investment terms must be distinct contexts. Negotiated rights, preferences, transfer constraints, capital calls, control and synergies require actual documents and assumptions. They cannot be inferred from an RHP or applied to ordinary IPO shares. Sources: S10, S11.

### 7. Governance evidence extends beyond a score

Ownership, related parties, board oversight and transparent material disclosures affect shareholder assessment. The product should expose unresolved conflicts and the evidence needed to investigate them, rather than convert a few governance ratios into a clean bill of health. Source: S12.

### 8. Evidence types must remain distinguishable

Professional communication guidance distinguishes facts and opinions and requires relevant limitations. The product should identify issuer statements, calculations, empirical observations, model estimates, assumptions and analyst interpretation separately. A practitioner citation supports a method, not this issuer's result. Source: S13.

### 9. Not every investor question is an econometric question

Historical comparisons can inform a thesis; qualitative claims require document and independent evidence. Short histories, selection and future-information leakage can invalidate model outputs. Product inference: every tool needs an input and applicability gate; model complexity is not a measure of analysis quality. Sources: S06, S14, S15. Do not require a regression merely to answer a business-description or contractual-rights question.

### 10. Disclosure rules require historical applicability

The February 2025 SEBI KPI circular is a relevant starting point for disclosure consistency. Its landing page and identity were verified; the full underlying PDF was unavailable through the browsing tool. Do not encode detailed statutory requirements from this research alone. Retrieve the applicable text and amendments before implementation; distinguish 2022/2023 examples from later requirements. Source: S16.

### 11. Interface evidence is part of analytical credibility

Status colors alone cannot communicate review states, and low-contrast citations defeat traceability. Require usable evidence controls, keyboard navigation and rendered contrast checks. Sources: S17, S18.

## Evidence deliberately not imported

- Gompers/Lerner's historical IPO study was identified, but fresh NBER page/PDF fetches returned 403. No effect size, return prediction or company survival probability is used.
- No commercial CMIE API/data dictionary license access was asserted. The product must inspect its actual licensed local schema; this research does not establish a complete CMIE attribute universe.
- No primary IPO source package was freshly audited for each exemplar during this PRD task. Previous review contradictions remain diligence tasks, not newly verified golden records.
- No validated Indian pre-IPO prediction model, user interviews, usability study, deployed UI acceptance or complete tool-integration inventory is claimed.
